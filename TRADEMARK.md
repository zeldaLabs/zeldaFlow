## What you can do, freely and without asking

- Use zeldaFlow, for anything, including at work and inside a commercial product
- Read, fork, and modify the source however you like
- Build and run your own builds, modified or not
- Ship a product built on this code — commercially, and closed-source if you want
- Say truthfully what your thing is built on: *"built on zeldaFlow"*,
  *"a fork of zeldaFlow"*, *"compatible with zeldaFlow"*
- Write about it, review it, teach it, screenshot it, put it in a talk
- Keep the marks intact in an **unmodified** copy you redistribute

That is genuinely most things. The list below is short on purpose.

## What needs a different name

**If you modify the code and distribute the result, give it your own name.**
Not zeldaFlow, and not a name a reasonable person would mistake for it —
"zeldaFlow Pro", "zeldaFlow+", "ZeldaFlow AI", "zFlow" and the like all read
as ours and are not available. Pick something that is yours.

Also don't:

- Use the three-wave mark, or a lookalike, as the icon of your fork or product
- Use **zeldaLabs** in your project name, org name, or domain
- Register a domain, app-store listing, package name, or social handle built
  around either mark
- Imply that zeldaLabs endorses, sponsors, maintains, or supports your fork —
  including by using the marks in a way that suggests it
- Recolor, tint, stretch, or redraw the logo. It is black on white. If you are
  showing it in an unmodified context, show it as supplied

The reason is narrow and not about control of the code: when someone downloads
something called zeldaFlow, they should be getting the thing zeldaLabs ships.
Your fork can be better than ours — it just has to be findable as yours when
it breaks.
